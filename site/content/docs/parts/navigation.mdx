Every app moves through the [app shell](../shell/index.mdx).
A view doesn't add navigation of its own, except a page tree or a list of chats in its own sidebar.
This page shows where each way of moving lives, and how to choose between drilling and nesting.

## Where things go

| Bar | What belongs in it |
| --- | --- |
| Title bar | The app icon, the history arrows, the scope path with its switchers, the lens box, status items, the window controls |
| Sidebar header | ↑ when there is a parent scope, the filter, collapse all, minimise |
| Sidebar | The current scope's places, with their counts |
| Sidebar foot | The `AccountMenu` |
| `ViewHeader` | The view's title or trail, the record switcher, the stepper, the view's actions |
| Panel header | The panel's name or trail, its real tabs, its actions, close |

Each thing goes in one bar, and the other bars don't repeat it.
A trail leaves out the title bar's path.
Panel headers leave out the rail's panels.
The view's header is its one bar, with no toolbar row under it.

## Structure and time

The path, the trails and the sidebar's ↑ are structure: each crumb goes up to what it names.
Back and forward are time, and the window keeps them once, in its [history](../shell/history.mdx).
No surface has a back button of its own.

| To go | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Up from a record to its list | The view's first crumb, or Escape |
| Up from a panel's drill | The panel's first crumb, or Escape |
| Up to the parent scope | The sidebar's ↑, or a path segment |
| To a sibling scope or record | The switcher on its path segment or crumb |
| Back to where the person was | ⌘[, Alt+←, the mouse's back button, the desktop arrow or the browser's |

Try each one from <DemoLink to="projects/atlas/build/issues/WEB-142">WEB-142</DemoLink>.

## Scope by drilling

Going into a domain, such as a space, a host or a project, drills the sidebar into that domain's places.
The domain is a segment of the path and of the address.
Entering it opens it as the person left it, with the lens worn last and the place that lens remembers.
The core and the plugins contribute places and lenses through `createShell`, and the shell reads them.
See [Sidebar](../shell/sidebar.mdx).

## Drill down or nest

Drill down when the target is a domain of its own with enough nested data to fill a view.
Projects drill down to the issues of a project and then to one issue.
The fleet drills down to a host and then to its sessions, files and logs.

Nest in place when the data is a part of one view.
Use saved views for a place's lists, and a tree for pages.
Choosing an item in place changes the view and keeps the tree.

To choose, ask whether the target has its own lists, its own panels and its own actions.
If it does, drill down.
If it is one more level of the same list, nest it.

## A view's own sidebar

A view with navigation of its own, such as a page tree or a list of chats, uses the sidebar of `ViewLayout`.

| Column | What goes in it | Width |
| --- | --- | --- |
| View sidebar | The view's own navigation | 180px to 420px, 240px at first |
| Main column | What the view shows | At least `minMain`, 560px by default |

The view sidebar resizes by its edge and collapses with its control.
Its width and fold are kept in the view layout's `storage`, or in the shell's store under `viewLayout/` and the layout's `id`.
When it lacks room beside the shell's sidebar, the shell folds its own sidebar to the rail until the view has the room.

- It collapses by itself when the main column would get narrower than `minMain`.
- Opened by hand, it docks beside the main column while the main column keeps 360px.
- Below that, it opens over the main column, with a scrim. Escape or a click on the scrim closes it.

The selection's details go in the shell's right panel, not in a column of the view.
See [Panels](../shell/panels.mdx).

## Selection and the trail

The Details panel shows the selection, and the trail stays as it is while the person selects rows.
Opening an item goes to it and changes the trail.
Give each row an open action.

## Phones

On a phone the sidebar is a drawer and the right panel covers the view.
The rail is hidden.
Each drill level is a screen of a `NavigationStack`.
The view header shows a back button named after the trail's parent crumb, and the crumbs and the stepper aren't shown.
Time is the system's back gesture.
List screens have a large title.
A view built from `ViewLayout` and `ViewHeader` gets this with no extra code.
See [Phones](../phones.mdx#navigation).
