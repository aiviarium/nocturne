A view is what the shell shows at a place.
The shell draws the window around it, and the view draws its header and its content from the parts on this page.

## ViewLayout

Use `ViewLayout` for every view, with a `ShellViewHeader` as its header.
The view area is raised on the canvas, square, and runs to the window's bottom edge.
The header is the view's one bar, 44px like every header in the window.

```tsx
<ViewLayout id="issues" header={<ShellViewHeader actions={actions}>{viewSwitch}</ShellViewHeader>}>
  …
</ViewLayout>
```

- At a place the header shows the place's title. At a record it shows the trail, `Issues / WEB-142 ⌄`, with the record crumb as a switcher and the stepper at the right. See [View header and trail](../shell/view-header.mdx).
- The title and the crumbs are at the drill size, the size of the title bar's path, so nothing changes size as the person drills.
- Header actions start with the icon actions, then the overflow menu, and end with the primary action.
- A view switch or search goes in the header's middle, as its children. There is no toolbar row under the header.
- The window's title comes from the header's title or last crumb.

### Width

Every view in an app uses the same width, so moving between them doesn't move the content.
The main column fills its width, and its content starts at its left edge.
Lists, tables, logs, forms and overviews all follow this rule.
A form lays out its fields in columns inside its sections instead of narrowing the view.

`ViewLayout` keeps its column widths and folds in its `storage`, or in the shell's store when it gets none.

`reading` is only for long prose, such as a document's body or an article.
It centres the column at a reading width.
Don't use it for forms or settings: one centred page among full-width pages reads as a different app.

## Tabs

Use segmented `Tabs` to switch between views of one place, in the view header's middle.
Use `variant="underline"` tabs to move between the sections of one record, with a `TabPanel` for each as children.

- Segmented tabs work like a radio group, with two to five short labels. They have no frame around them.
- Underline tabs show a count after the label when it's above zero.
- The selected tab takes the neutral `active` fill and `text-1`, in both variants. It has no underline or bar, and its weight stays.
- The arrow keys, Home and End move and select.

## Card

Use a `Card` for a box of content in a view, such as an idea, a figure or a group of settings.
It has 12px padding (`--nc-pad`), `--radius-md`, a `line-soft` edge and a `bg-1` fill.
A `nested` card is `bg-2`.

- A card is as tall as its content, and cards in a row of a grid share its height.
- `fill` makes it use a box that a grid gives it, and the body takes the height. A `RowList` scrolls inside it and an `EmptyState` centres in it. A card with one `Stat` keeps its label and figure at the top.
- `title`, `count` and `action` put a `SectionHeading` inside.
- `padding="none"` is for media, tables and setting rows that reach the edges.
- `href` or `onClick` make the whole card one link.
- A selected card takes a `line-strong` ring.
- A card inside a card, or a list in a card, doesn't add a second padding.

## Panel

Use a `Panel` for a tool region with a header inside a view, such as a host's meters on a dashboard.
The shell's docked panels are `ShellPanel`, see [Panels](../shell/panels.mdx).

- The header is 44px, with the title, a `state` and `actions`.
- The body has 12px padding.
- `footer` is only for totals or pagination.
- `fill` works as for `Card`.
- `flush` is for a table or a terminal.

## SectionHeading

Use `SectionHeading` for every section inside a view or a card.
It is 13px semibold `text-1`, at the height of a small control.

- `count` is a total in `text-3`.
- One `action` goes at the right. It is a ghost `Button size="sm"`, an `IconButton size="sm"`, a link or a `State`.
- Group labels in a sidebar or a menu are small labels, 11px in `text-3`. They aren't section headings.

## Split and SplitGroup

Use `Split` for two columns in a view with a divider between them, such as a list and the reader of its selected item.
Use `SplitGroup` for more than two.

- The divider is a focusable separator. The arrow keys, Home and End move it, and a double click resets it.
- On a phone the columns stack.
- The selection's details go in the shell's Details panel, not in a column of the view. A split is for a reader the view itself is about.

## Dock, Frame and TabStrip

Use `Dock` when a view's content is several tiled frames with tabs, such as a host's terminals side by side.
It is a view's content, never a part of the shell, and the shell's panels have no tiling.

- `Frame` is one tile, with a `TabStrip` as its header. A `bare` frame has no edge, radius or fill, for a lone log or terminal that runs to the view's edges.
- On a phone a dock has no split buttons.
- `TabStrip` is a row of document tabs with icons, titles and close buttons. Tabs shrink, then the strip scrolls, and "All tabs" lists them with a filter.
- Dragging a tab onto another frame's strip moves it there. Dropping it on a side of a frame, inside a `TabDropFrame`, splits that frame.
- The layout is data. `dockLayout`, `dockAdd`, `dockSplit`, `dockRemove` and `dockSync` change it, and the app keeps it.

## LinkProvider

`LinkProvider` sends the links of `NavItem` and `Button` through the app's router.
Put it around the parts whose links stay inside the app.
The demo's <DemoLink to="projects/atlas/product/roadmap">Roadmap</DemoLink> uses it.

## Where things belong

The project is the hub.
Issues, docs, agent sessions and environments sit inside it, and each module adds its part there.
A module doesn't get a section of its own.

<DoDont do="design/project-hub" dont="design/plugin-silos" />

The title bar's status items show only while they matter, such as a lost connection or a failing job.
They don't list the things that are fine.
