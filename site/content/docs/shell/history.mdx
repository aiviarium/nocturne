The window keeps one history.
Trails in the headers are structure and go up.
The history is time and goes back to where the person was.

## Stops

A stop is any move the person makes, to a scope, lens, place, saved view, record or panel drill.
Each stop is a real entry of the router, carrying its index.
⌘[ and ⌘], Alt+← and Alt+→, the mouse's back and forward buttons and, in a browser tab, the browser's own arrows all walk the same list.
With a `storage`, a reload keeps it.

- Lens switches in a row, in one scope, fold into one stop. Switching back to the lens the run began in undoes the run.
- A lens switch waits while the previous move is still landing, then applies the latest one.
- A short address, such as `/projects/atlas`, means "the lens worn last there". Its meaning changes as the person moves.
The history writes each stop's address out in full when it records it.

## The arrows

In a desktop window, `HistoryArrows` is right after the app icon, before the path.
It is two 20px chevrons in `text-3`.

- Each tooltip names where it goes: "Back to WEB-139, Details › WEB-127".
- A long press or a right click lists the last ten stops on that side.
- An arrow with no step dims.

In a browser tab there are no arrows.
The browser's own do the same job.

## Routers

`createHistory` takes a router, which is the only thing that touches the address.

| Router | For |
| --- | --- |
| `memoryRouter(initial)` | Tests, examples and a desktop window without an address bar |
| `hashRouter()` | A browser tab, with the app's address in the hash: `#/projects/atlas` |
| The app's own `HistoryRouter` | Any other router: `path`, `state`, `push`, `replace`, `go` and `subscribe` |

`createShell` makes its history over the `router` it is given.
An app that only needs the history uses `createHistory({ router, describe, storage })`, where `describe` names an address for the arrows' tooltips and the list.

## Going up instead

On WEB-139 with Details at WEB-127, each control gives the same answer however the person got there:

- the panel's "WEB-139" crumb goes back to WEB-139 in the panel
- the view's "Issues" crumb, or Escape, goes to Issues
- the sidebar's ↑ goes to Projects
