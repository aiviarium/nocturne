`TitleBar` is 44px across the whole window.
It has three zones.

| Zone | From left to right |
| --- | --- |
| Left | The app icon, the history arrows in a desktop window, the scope path |
| Centre | The scope box, on the window's centre |
| Right | The app's status items, then the window controls |

Every text in the bar is at the drill size, 12px/500 on a 20px line, on one baseline.
In a desktop window the bar is the drag region, marked with `data-drag-region` or the attribute in `dragAttribute`.
`Shell` builds the bar from the registry.
The app passes only `icon`, `appMenu`, `status` and `window` to `Shell`.

## The app icon

The icon is the app's mark alone, with no chip or tinted box behind it.
With `appMenu` it is a menu button that opens the application menu.
The path starts right after it, or after the arrows, with the normal gap.

## The scope path

`ScopePath` names the scopes from the workspace down to the one the window shows, with "/" between them.
The workspace and the current scope read in `text-1`.
The segments between go up and are quiet `text-3` text.
Only the colour tells them apart, and size and weight stay the same.

A segment for one of a kind, such as a project or a host, has a caret that opens a `Switcher`.
The switcher is in the filter format, with a focused field ("Switch project…"), sections, each entry's mark, and the current one checked.
Typing narrows the list, the arrow keys and Enter pick, and Escape clears the field and then closes.
Every path segment and every record crumb opens the same switcher at the same size.

## The scope box

`ScopeBox` sits on the window's absolute centre, not after the path.
It moves only when it would come within 16px of the path or the right-hand items, and then just enough to clear them.
With still no room, the path folds its leading segments into "…", then the lenses drop their labels, then they fold one by one into the box's `+N` menu.
The lens that is on always stays.

The magnifier is at the box's left end, then the lenses: `⌕ │ Explore │ Product │ Build │ +1 ⌄`.
A scope without lenses has a search field in the box instead, "Search Docs…" with its shortcut.

## Lenses

A lens is a way of looking at a scope, with its own places, panels and hue.
`LensSwitch` draws a scope's lenses as a segmented control.
The lens that is on is a raised neutral segment with its icon in full hue.
The others are quiet text with their icons in a muted mix of their hue, brighter on hover.

- ⌘1 to ⌘9 put on the shown lenses in order.
- The `⌄` menu lists the hidden lenses and opens Customise, where each lens can be hidden or moved by dragging, or by Alt and an arrow key.
- A lens a plugin adds with `pinned: false` starts hidden.
- Each lens remembers its place, so switching back returns to where the person was in it.

## The tint line

A 1px line runs under the bar.
It takes the hue of the lens that is on, strongest under that lens's segment, and fades out over 32px at the window's edges.
Its bright spot jumps to the new lens on a switch, and its hue cross-fades in 100ms.
A scope without lenses, or the person's choice in [Appearance](./appearance.mdx), gives the plain neutral hairline.

## The command palette

⌘K or the magnifier turns the box into `CommandPalette` in place.
The field starts with the scope's chips and the lens that is on.

- Typing searches the scope.
- `:` lists the scope's lenses, and `>` the actions: the scope's commands, the panels and the workspace's commands.
- The arrow keys move, Enter runs, and Escape clears the field and then closes.
- Backspace in an empty field drops the last chip, which widens the search.

The focused field has a 3px glow in the accent, which is its focus ring.

## Status items

`status` takes `TitleBarItem`s: an icon and a few words at the drill size, quiet until hovered.
A `tone` colours text that needs attention, such as "1 failing".
Show an item only while it matters.

## Window controls

`WindowControls` is always at the bar's right end, on every platform, with nothing after it.
The library draws it in the `mac` or `win` style.
The mac dots are neutral and take their colours only while the group is hovered, and close turns red on Windows.
The app passes the handlers that talk to its native window (`onMinimise`, `onMaximise`, `onClose`, `maximised`).
The library calls nothing itself.

## Desktop window or browser tab

`runsIn` says who draws back and forward.

- `desktop`, the default, shows the history arrows after the app icon and the window controls at the right. The bar is the drag region.
- `browser`: no arrows and no window controls. The browser's own arrows walk the same history.
